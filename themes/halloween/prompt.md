Halloween Pigeon — Image Transformation Prompt

Objective

Transform the provided photograph of a pigeon into a humorous, photorealistic Halloween portrait.

Dress the pigeon in {{COSTUME}}, complemented by {{PROPS}}.

If the photograph shows more than one pigeon, dress every clearly visible pigeon. The most prominent pigeon wears {{COSTUME}} with {{PROPS}}. Each other pigeon wears its own different, simple homemade Halloween costume, so no two match. Every rule below applies to each pigeon.

The central creative principle is understated visual absurdity: an ordinary pigeon, in an ordinary environment, inexplicably dressed for Halloween.

The result should look like an authentic photograph rather than a staged or digitally fabricated scene.

1. Preserve the Original Photograph

Image preservation is the highest priority.

Treat the uploaded photograph as the source of truth. Modify only what is necessary to add the Halloween costume and props convincingly.

* Preserve the pigeon's distinctive appearance, including feather colors, patterns, eyes, beak, proportions, and anatomy.
* Preserve the original environment, including architecture, vegetation, background objects, weather, and season.
* Maintain the original lighting, shadows, perspective, camera angle, and depth of field.
* Match the original color mode and grade. If the photograph is black-and-white, sepia, or heavily color-graded, render every added item in that same mode. A black-and-white photo stays entirely black-and-white.
* Preserve the pigeon's original pose, gaze, and expression wherever possible.
* Do not relocate the pigeon or introduce a new background.
* Do not add pumpkins, cobwebs, fog, Halloween decorations, or other environmental elements that were not present in the original photograph.
* Avoid unnecessary changes to the image's composition.

Minor adjustments to the pigeon's pose, head position, or framing are permitted only when they materially improve the costume's appearance or the understated absurdity of the scene.

2. Natural Pigeon Appearance and Behavior

Let the pigeon look and behave like an ordinary pigeon.

* Preserve its natural expression and gaze.
* Do not intentionally make the pigeon appear serious, grumpy, confused, happy, excited, or scary.
* Avoid anthropomorphic facial expressions, gestures, or poses.
* Maintain realistic bird anatomy and proportions.
* Allow the pigeon's existing appearance and posture to contribute naturally to the humor.

The pigeon does not need to communicate a particular emotion.

The costume is the joke, not the pigeon's expression.

3. The Costume

Dress the pigeon in {{COSTUME}}.

* Render the costume as a real, slightly homemade miniature Halloween costume, with authentic material texture: visible seams and weave for fabric, felt, satin, or burlap; believable stiffness for hats, masks, and collars.
* Fit the costume naturally around the pigeon's body, accommodating its wings and anatomy.
* Keep the pigeon's head, eyes, beak, and distinctive natural features visible, even under a mask, hood, or sheet, unless the costume itself says it covers the face.
* Do not add any text, lettering, tags, or labels to the costume.

4. Props

Add exactly these props to the most prominent pigeon: {{PROPS}}. Do not add others. Other pigeons in a group may wear one small prop that suits their own costume.

* Props should look like real miniature costume pieces, worn by or attached to the bird.
* Props must stay on the pigeon. Do not place them in the scene around it.
* Avoid obscuring the pigeon's eyes, beak, or distinctive features.

5. Photographic Realism

The transformed image must be convincingly photorealistic.

* Match the original photograph's lighting, exposure, shadows, color temperature, and color mode. Added items take on the photo's color treatment, including black-and-white, sepia, or film grading, along with its grain and contrast.
* Preserve realistic feather textures and iridescence.
* Render costume materials with detailed stitching, natural folds, and believable contact with the bird.
* Ensure the costume follows the pigeon's anatomy.
* Preserve the original background's depth of field and photographic characteristics.
* Avoid illustration, cartoon styling, CGI aesthetics, artificial-looking feathers, or exaggerated proportions.

The costume should appear to have been physically present when the original photograph was taken.

6. Creative Direction

The intended humor is subtle, absurd, and ironic.

Imagine encountering an otherwise ordinary pigeon in its natural environment, except that it happens to be wearing a Halloween costume.

Do not manufacture comedy or spookiness through exaggerated expressions, theatrical poses, eerie lighting, or additional visual gags.

Allow the contrast between the ordinary photograph and the ridiculous costume to create the humor.

Exercise creativity primarily through the costume's materials, colors, and small handmade details that reward closer inspection.

7. Final Output

Generate one high-quality, photorealistic image.

Requirements:

* Preserve the pigeon's recognizable identity and natural appearance.
* Preserve the original environment and photographic composition as closely as possible.
* Preserve the pigeon's original expression, gaze, and posture unless a minor adjustment clearly improves the result.
* Dress the pigeon in {{COSTUME}}.
* In a group, dress every clearly visible pigeon, each in a different costume.
* Match the original photograph's color mode: in a black-and-white photo, the costume is black-and-white too.
* Add {{PROPS}}, and nothing else.
* Maintain believable anatomy, material textures, lighting, and shadows.
* Do not introduce unrelated objects, environmental changes, text, captions, tags, watermarks, or borders.

Success criterion: The result should feel like the original photograph, with the smallest possible set of changes needed to make the pigeon look absurdly ready for Halloween. The humor should emerge naturally from that contrast.
